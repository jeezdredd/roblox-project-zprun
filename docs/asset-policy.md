# Asset policy — animation, audio, models

Permanent rules for every stage of this project. Referenced from `CLAUDE.md`.
Revised 2026-09-07: the policy now judges **results**, not methods.

## Why this exists

The first version banned code-authored animation and any synthesised audio, because
early attempts produced work that had to be thrown away and nobody could see the
output while making it. That is no longer the situation: Studio MCP gives a screenshot
loop, Blender and RBXMonkey are wired in, and AI generation for sound and mocap has
become good enough to be one more tool on the bench.

The goal is animation that reads as alive rather than stock Roblox, and audio that
sits in the mix. Any technique that gets there is allowed. What stays fixed is
provenance: every asset has a licence we can show, and nothing enters the project
that we do not have the right to ship.

## Rules

1. **Any technique, judged by eye and ear.** Authored clips, mocap, retargeted
   libraries, AI-generated motion, Luau-authored poses, procedural layers, IK,
   blends of all of the above — all allowed, for every rig. The gate is a playtest
   in Studio: it looks right in motion, or it goes back. The same applies to audio:
   recorded, licensed, layered in a DAW, AI-generated, or procedurally shaped in code,
   as long as it passes a listen in context. Keep `NEEDED.md` for slots nobody has
   had time to fill, not as a substitute for trying.

2. **Layer, do not choose.** The livest results come from stacking: a base clip for
   the gait, a procedural layer for weight and breath, IK for contact, and impulses
   for hits. Prefer this over hunting for one perfect clip. `Motor6D.Transform`
   written in `RunService.Stepped` lands on top of the `Animator` pass; keep that order.

3. **Provenance is not negotiable.** Every asset has a manifest entry with a source
   and a licence, and `assets/LICENSES.md` is regenerated by `scripts/sync_configs.py`.
   Allowed:
   - Own work, in any tool.
   - CC0, public domain, CC-BY with the attribution recorded in `LICENSES.md`.
   - Paid or free packs whose licence permits use in a shipped game on any engine,
     including packs bought on Fab or the Unity Asset Store **when the individual
     asset's licence is not engine-bound** — read the licence text, not the storefront.
   - Roblox Creator Store items, including AI-generated ones (Cube 3D and similar)
     made under the owner's account.
   - AI-generated audio, motion, models and textures from services whose terms grant
     commercial rights to the output (record the service and the terms version).
   - Mixamo, Sonniss GameAudioGDC, Freesound, OpenGameArt, Kenney, as before.

   Not allowed, ever: assets extracted from other games (see `docs/research/` — those
   files are reference only and never leave `Downloads`), storefront packs whose
   licence names a specific engine, anything whose licence we cannot locate.

4. **Never invent an assetId.** Ids come only from a real upload through the manifest
   pipeline or from the Creator Store after the owner has inserted the asset. This
   is correctness, not caution: a guessed id fails silently at runtime.

5. **A placeholder is better than a hole, if it is labelled.** A slot may temporarily
   carry a stand-in from an unrelated category while the real asset is in progress,
   as long as the manifest entry is marked `"status": "placeholder"` and the slot is
   in `NEEDED.md`. Placeholders never ship: `scripts/refresh_status.py` should fail a
   release build while any remain.

## Animation

### What goes where

| Rig | Base | Layers on top |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Viewmodel (first person) | Procedural: springs, `PoseLibrary`, `AnimationSequencer` | Recoil, sway, breath, strafe tilt; authored clips for reload and inspect where they read better |
| Player body (third person) | Clips: Blender, Mixamo, mocap, AI motion | Foot IK, lean from `GlanceController`, procedural breath |
| Zombies | Clips, several variants per state, random phase offset | Head look, alert turn, knockback impulses, ragdoll on death |
| Cinematic | Clips for bodies, code for camera and timing | — |

Roblox binds animation by joint name. Zombies and the player body stay on the
standard R15 joint set; the zombie rig is the reduced R15 described below. Custom
skeletons need a stated reason in `wiki/project-zprun/Decisions.md`.

The zombie rig is a **reduced R15**: R15 joint names (`Root`, `Waist`, `Neck`,
shoulders, hips) on the parts those joints drive, without elbows, wrists, knees or
ankles. Full R15 clips play on it unchanged because Roblox drops tracks for missing
joints. This keeps per-zombie cost low enough for the densities the run needs.

### Pipelines

- Blender → Roblox: `tools/animation_pipeline/` (Mixamo retarget) or RBXMonkey for
  hand-authored work. Export a KeyframeSequence, upload as `Animation` through
  `scripts/upload_assets.py` (content type `model/x-rbxm`), record the id.
- AI motion (text-to-motion, video mocap) goes through the same Blender retarget
  step; it is a source of clips, not a shortcut past the rig.
- Every `Animator:LoadAnimation` reads its id from `AssetIds.luau`, never a literal.

### Quality gate

- No bone teleports; transitions blend with `FadeTime >= 0.15`.
- Loops have no seam.
- Procedural layers do not fight the clip: `Transform` after the Animator pass.
- Crowd states use several clip variants or a random phase offset; a synchronised
  horde is a bug.
- Event audio comes from `KeyframeMarker`, not a wall-clock timer.
- Checked in a Studio playtest, from the camera the player actually uses.

## Audio

Sources: anything under rule 3. Generation is fine when it passes the gate below.
Procedural shaping in code (layering, pitch and volume randomisation, ducking, the
Audio API effect graph) is encouraged; it is what makes a small library sound large.

### Quality gate

- Footsteps, gunshots and bites have at least 3 variants.
- Levels are consistent within a class; nothing sticks out by roughly 10 dB.
- RollOff matches the event scale; 3D sounds do not carry across the map.
- Every file is in the manifest with a licence.

## Models and textures

Same provenance rule. Own work, licensed packs, Creator Store, AI generation with
commercial rights. Every mesh goes through the GLB → Open Cloud → Studio import path
in `tools/cube3d/README.md` so the id lands in the manifest. Textures follow the
ambientCG pattern already in `assets/textures/`.

## Division of labour

**Claude Code:** slots, configs, pipeline scripts, integration by id, procedural
layers, `NEEDED.md`, owner instructions, and — new — first passes at code-authored
poses and generated audio, each verified by screenshot or by playing it in Studio
before it is presented as done.

**Owner:** final say on look and sound, downloads and purchases, Blender work, uploads
through the manifest script, Creator Store inserts, licence reading.

The trap this document used to guard against was generating something because it
was the quick option. The trap now is shipping something because it exists. Judge
it in motion, in the mix, from the player's seat.
